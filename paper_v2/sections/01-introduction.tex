\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Agents and assistants act on records that change: clearance is revoked, a ticket rerouted, a preference
replaced. Earlier entries remain visible, but only the current state should govern the action. Direct
retrieval studies show that old values intrude on current-value reports, especially after repeated
updates \citep{wang2026unable,chattaraj2026dual}. Prior work also tests downstream use: STALE evaluates
implicit policy adaptation \citep{chao2026stale}, and \citet{guo2026context} include decisions under
revised constraints. We ask whether obsolete words affect policy decisions on histories with correct
retrieval, and whether supersession matters beyond the word's presence near the queried entity.

Supersession-specific interference would motivate state-tracking interventions, such as historical
marking or attention routing \citep{guo2026context}. Similar interference from incidental mentions
would instead suggest a broader lexical effect, analogous to contextual entrainment \citep{niu2025entrainment}.
Here the measured outputs are policy-derived action labels, so the analogy does not establish a shared
mechanism. Either account motivates matched mention controls and decision-level evaluation; we test no
mitigation.

\paragraph{Design.} We hold the current state, policy, query and correct action fixed, and edit one
obsolete state to imply either the wrong or the correct action. The paired difference in decision
preference and generated answers measures interference (Figure~\ref{fig:example}). Matched controls keep
the edited word and its position but change its role: an updated other attribute, an incidental entity
mention, or an unattached word. Direct queries check current and initial retrieval on the same records.

\paragraph{Findings.} Qwen3-8B was tested on held-out wording under a protocol frozen before confirmation;
four further models used identical rows under dated amendments. Compact estimates use frozen-gate-selected
cells; confirmation-selected and tracked-history analyses are post hoc robustness checks.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Obsolete states shift decision preferences.} Routing shifts were 1.085--4.785 nats across
  five models and persisted in histories with correct current and initial retrieval
  (\S\ref{sec:results-h1}, \S\ref{sec:results-replication}).
  \item \textbf{Generated decisions vary across tested conditions.} In Qwen3-8B, the superseded net
  switch rate was 0.185 on the original routing grid and 0.031 in an exploratory run where the other
  entity's state supported the correct action. Different histories make this comparison descriptive.
  Thinking-enabled generation produced no superseded switches in either Qwen3 model
  (\S\ref{sec:results-conditions}).
  \item \textbf{Interference requires neither supersession nor queried-attribute assignment.} Incidental
  mentions shifted preferences in every model. A never-updated other attribute was unresolved from, or
  exceeded, the superseded assignment in the two Qwen3 models. Mention-versus-superseded switch
  differences varied by model (\S\ref{sec:results-h2}, \S\ref{sec:results-replication}).
  \item \textbf{Easy-query sensitivity did not improve failure prediction} beyond the specified
  pre-decision features in any model (\S\ref{sec:results-h3}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is and is not new.} That stale context interferes with answers is established
\citep{wang2026unable,guo2026context,qian2026forget,chao2026stale}, as is the fact that mentioned tokens
gain probability \citep{niu2025entrainment}. Our contribution is the controlled contrast between superseded assignments and matched
other-attribute and incidental-mention controls while holding the correct policy action fixed. We report scores, generated answers and retrieval checks, a negative result for
sensitivity-based prediction, and measurement problems involving chat-format events and reduced-precision
scoring (Appendix~\ref{app:history}).
