\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In five open models, obsolete states shift routing preferences toward the action they imply, including
histories with correct current and initial retrieval. The interference requires neither supersession
nor assignment to the queried attribute: incidental entity mentions shift preferences in every model
and change more decisions than superseded assignments in two. Generated switches were less frequent
in an exploratory Qwen3-8B run where the other entity's state supported the correct action, but the
comparison uses different histories and does not isolate cue agreement. Thinking-enabled generation
produced no superseded switches in either Qwen3 model. Retrieval-score sensitivity did not improve
prediction beyond the specified pre-decision features. These findings motivate evaluating stale-context
benchmarks and mitigations with incidental-mention controls, explicit variation of other record cues,
and decision-level outcomes alongside retrieval accuracy. \modelcountconclusion
