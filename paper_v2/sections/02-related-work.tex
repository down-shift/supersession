\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Interference from updated values.} Proactive interference grows with the number of overwrites,
and errors are mostly earlier values of the same key \citep{wang2026unable}. Models retrieve the first value
of a key more reliably than the latest \citep{chattaraj2026dual,qiao2026retrieval}. \citet{guo2026context}
name the failure \emph{stale binding}. They find that the current value stays linearly recoverable on
failures, that historical wording reduces errors, and that attention routing repairs most of them. Their
decision scenarios, with closed reasoning models, showed no difference between full histories and
resolved snapshots. Benchmarks of forgetting and stale memory evaluate dialogue and agent settings by
accuracy \citep{qian2026forget,chao2026stale}. STALE separates recognizing an update from applying it. We
use a single update, decisions fixed by an explicit policy, and matched minimal pairs with lexical
controls, below the interference loads at which retrieval breaks.

\paragraph{Entrainment and distraction.} Tokens that appear in the context gain probability whether or
not they are relevant \citep{niu2025entrainment}, and irrelevant context degrades reasoning
\citep{shi2023distracted}. Our mention and unattached-word constructions measure this in the update
setting. Counterfactual edits are compound treatments that need matched alternative edits as baselines
\citep{yang2026compared}, which is how our contrasts are built.

\paragraph{Binding, state tracking and conflicts.} Mechanistic accounts of entity binding
\citep{feng2024bind,gurarieh2026mixing,prakash2026lookbacks} and state tracking
\citep{tang2026entities,kim2023entity} may explain why lexical identity matters; we do not test them. Our records contain an intra-context
conflict with an explicit temporal resolution \citep{xu2024conflicts}. Questions about the value valid at a
given time \citep{yang2026conflicts} correspond to our historical retrieval check, not to our decision task.
