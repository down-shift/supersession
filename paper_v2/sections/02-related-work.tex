\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Interference from updated values.} Proactive interference grows with the number of overwrites,
and errors are mostly earlier values of the same key \citep{wang2026unable}. Models retrieve the first value
of a key more reliably than the latest \citep{chattaraj2026dual,qiao2026retrieval}. \citet{guo2026context}
name the failure \emph{stale binding}. They find that the current value stays linearly recoverable on
failures, that historical wording reduces errors, and that attention routing repairs most of them. In
their tested full-history decision conditions, reasoning models produced no interpretable nonoptimal
actions, although output validity and control-condition performance varied (their Appendix F.4).
Forgetting benchmarks evaluate dialogue and agent settings \citep{qian2026forget,chao2026stale}; STALE
explicitly tests downstream use through implicit policy adaptation. Our contribution is the contrast
between superseded assignments and matched other-attribute and incidental-mention controls, with the
correct policy action held fixed.

\paragraph{Entrainment and distraction.} Tokens already present in a prompt gain probability even when
irrelevant \citep{niu2025entrainment}; irrelevant context also degrades reasoning \citep{shi2023distracted}.
Our controls concern policy-derived action labels, rather than direct repetition of the edited word.
Counterfactual edits can bundle an intended manipulation with incidental surface-form changes
\citep{yang2026compared}. Their benign-perturbation baselines motivate comparisons with alternative edits;
our constructions instead change the word's semantic role, and their syntactic differences prevent
attributing the contrasts solely to supersession.

\paragraph{Binding, state tracking and conflicts.} Mechanistic studies of binding and belief tracking
\citep{feng2024bind,gurarieh2026mixing,prakash2026lookbacks} and state tracking \citep{tang2026entities}
complement behavioral entity-tracking studies \citep{kim2023entity}. These offer candidate explanations,
which we do not test. Our records contain an intra-context conflict with explicit temporal resolution
\citep{xu2024conflicts}. The temporal-conflict subset of \citet{yang2026conflicts} tests time-dependent
claim validity, corresponding most closely to our historical retrieval check.
