\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers in this section come from the frozen confirmation (Qwen3-8B, held-out template, 384 histories
per task) unless marked otherwise. We give intervals only for the primary estimates; ``not resolved'' means
that the interval includes zero. All intervals are in the tables of Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

On the confirmation data, the routing cells meet all competence criteria and the access cells do not, mainly because current-only
decisions fall short of 0.90 (Table~\ref{tab:competence}). Asked for the \emph{initial} state on the
superseded items, the model answered correctly in 0.990 of routing and 0.973 of access cases. In routing,
then, the model reports the current state, reports the initial state, and applies the policy without
history at or above 0.95. Access results are reported below as fixed-grid averages over cells that do not
meet the competence criteria.

\subsection{Obsolete states shift scores and decisions}
\label{sec:results-h1}

H1 is supported in both tasks. In routing, when the superseded state implied the wrong action rather
than the correct one, the wrong action's log-odds rose by $D_{\mathrm{S}} = 3.133$ nats
(97.5\% interval $[2.743, 3.516]$). In access, where cells do not meet the competence criteria, the shift
was $3.904$. For scale, editing the \emph{current} state itself, which legitimately
changes the correct action, moved the same quantity by $-8.197$ (routing) and $-10.100$ nats (access).

The shift reaches generated answers. In routing, the net switch rate toward the wrong action was $0.185$
$[0.143, 0.227]$: 75 histories switched to the wrong action and 4 to the correct one. In each routing
cell, it was between 0.164 and 0.227 (Table~\ref{tab:switch-cells}). In access it was $0.167$ (65 against 1). Figure~\ref{fig:competence} in Appendix~\ref{app:tables} shows the same effect as accuracy. In every
routing cell, decisions were correct in 0.906--0.953 of histories when the superseded state implied the
correct action, and in 0.727--0.750 when it implied the wrong one.

\paragraph{Histories the model demonstrably tracks.} Aggregate retrieval accuracy does not show that the
switching histories are ones the model reads correctly. In a post hoc analysis, defined after the
Qwen3-8B confirmation results and applied unchanged to Qwen3-14B, we kept a history only if three
conditions held on the same records: the model retrieved the current state correctly under both members
of the pair, retrieved the initial state correctly under both superseded members, and decided the
current-only item correctly. This kept 348 of 384 routing histories for the superseded construction
(Table~\ref{tab:joint} in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). In them, the net switch rate toward the wrong action was $0.158$
(58 histories to the wrong action, 3 to the correct one) and the preference shift $2.990$ nats. The effect is therefore not carried by histories in which the model misreads the record. In
access, the same restriction keeps 284 of 384 histories and gives a net switch rate of $0.176$ (50 against 0). In every one of these histories, though, the other entity's current state
also implies the wrong action; \S\ref{sec:results-conditions} removes that cue.



\subsection{Attribute and mention contrasts}
\label{sec:results-h2}

\input{data/tables/tracked}

This section reports Qwen3-8B; \S\ref{sec:results-replication} gives the other models. H2 compares the
superseded assignment with an assignment of the same word to a different attribute that was later updated. It is supported in routing, $\Delta_{\mathrm{SO}} = +1.224$ (97.5\% interval $[0.973, 1.485]$), with
generated switches agreeing (paired difference $+0.120$). In routing, then, a superseded
assignment to the queried attribute produces more interference than an updated assignment to an
unrelated attribute. In access
(non-competent cells) neither the contrast ($+0.035$) nor its switch difference is resolved.

The mention control, a secondary outcome, shows that supersession is not the largest source of pressure
in Qwen3-8B.
When the same entity only \emph{mentioned} the word, the effect was larger than for the superseded
assignment. In routing, $D_{\mathrm{M}} = 4.807$ against $3.133$, so
$\Delta_{\mathrm{SM}} = -1.675$. Net switch rates were $0.294$ (113 to wrong, 0 to
correct) against $0.185$, a paired difference (M $-$ S) of $+0.109$. In access, $D_{\mathrm{M}} = 5.182$ against
$3.904$ ($\Delta_{\mathrm{SM}} = -1.278$); the switch difference there is not resolved. A word attached to no entity still shifted preference
substantially, by $2.496$ (routing) and $3.043$ nats (access), but rarely changed a generated decision
($0.039$ and $0.062$). On the routing histories tracked under all four constructions (Table~\ref{tab:tracked}; post hoc), the mention
exceeded the superseded assignment in net switch rate, and the superseded assignment exceeded the updated unrelated
attribute; the unrelated attribute and the unattached word were not resolved.

\paragraph{An other attribute that is never updated.} A fifth construction, N, added by a dated amendment before
its data were scored, removes the later update of the other attribute (a filler line of the same length
replaces it) on the 384 routing confirmation histories of both Qwen3 models (Table~\ref{tab:static-other} in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). As predicted, the update reduced interference ($\Delta_{\mathrm{ON}} =
-1.204$ and $-1.942$ nats). Never updated, the other attribute was not resolved from the superseded
assignment in Qwen3-8B ($\Delta_{\mathrm{SN}} = +0.020$, switches $-0.008$) and interfered more in Qwen3-14B
($-1.076$, switches $-0.096$). $\Delta_{\mathrm{SO}}$ thus compares the queried attribute with one whose value
was later replaced; it does not show that assignment to the queried attribute matters.

Across four earlier, non-confirmatory splits, $\Delta_{\mathrm{SM}}$ never favored the superseded
assignment, but its size varied with the template (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}, Figure~\ref{fig:replication}).

\subsection{Easy-query sensitivity did not improve failure prediction}
\label{sec:results-h3}

The edit strongly affected easy retrieval scores: the mean $R_{\mathrm{easy}}$ on superseded items was
$8.096$ nats in access and $10.878$ in routing. That sensitivity did not improve prediction beyond the
specified pre-decision features under the specified logistic model (Table~\ref{tab:h3} in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). Pre-decision features reduced log loss from $0.414$ to
$0.346$ and reached AUROC $0.780$. Adding $R_{\mathrm{easy}}$ reduced log loss by $0.0004$ nats (not resolved); the upper end of its interval is
under 3\% of what the simple features already gain. The
result is the same in each task, for the wrong-answer outcome, and in every further model.

\subsection{When score shifts change decisions}
\label{sec:results-conditions}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/conditions.pdf}
\caption{Qwen3-8B, routing, per construction, with 95\% intervals. Thinking mode has no scores.}
\label{fig:conditions}
\end{figure}

Two further runs on Qwen3-8B ask when the score shift becomes a different decision
(Figure~\ref{fig:conditions}; Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). In the main design, confusing the two entities gives the wrong action, because
the other entity's current state implies it. A balanced subset, recorded before its data were scored, sets
the other entity's current state to the queried entity's, so that it implies the correct action (192 new
routing histories, held-out template; Appendix~\ref{app:history}). The model retrieved both states and
decided correctly without history in every balanced history. $D_{\mathrm{S}}$ stayed positive
($3.895$), but the net switch rate fell to $0.031$ (6 histories to wrong, none to correct), against $0.185$
in the main design. The mention kept a net switch rate of $0.177$ (34 against 0). In this model, then, the superseded assignment shifts scores with or without
the second cue, but mostly changes decisions when that cue also points to the wrong action; the mention
depends less on it. The balanced histories differ from the main ones, and identical current states may make
the decision easier, so the comparison is descriptive.

Second, we generated answers with thinking enabled on the main-design routing rows (greedy, at most 4,096
new tokens; no scores). In Qwen3-8B, current-only accuracy was $1.000$ and 2 of 3,456 items were invalid. No
history switched for the superseded, other-attribute or unattached constructions; the mention switched 7 to
the wrong action ($0.018$). In Qwen3-14B (current-only accuracy $1.000$, none invalid), only
2 other-attribute and 1 mention histories switched, all to the wrong action. Thinking and direct answers are
different generation processes, and no test was prespecified.

\label{sec:results-replication}
\label{sec:results-gemma}

Four further models were run under amendments dated before any of their data were scored, with the same
rows, rules and runtime (Appendix~\ref{app:history}): Qwen3-14B, then Gemma 4 12B, Qwen3.5 9B and Granite 4.1
8B. Table~\ref{tab:cross-model} and Figure~\ref{fig:models} give each on the cells competent on the confirmation data: all three routing
cells for Qwen3-14B, two for the others, and access only for Gemma 4 12B.

The obsolete-state effect replicates in every model. In routing, $D_{\mathrm{S}}$ is
1.072--4.785 nats, and the net switch rate in tracked histories is 0.143--0.233, with at most 3 histories
switching to the correct action (Table~\ref{tab:joint-by-model}). $\Delta_{\mathrm{SO}}$ is positive in
every model; its paired switch difference is resolved in Gemma 4 12B and Qwen3.5 9B, not in Qwen3-14B or
Granite 4.1 8B. The mention comparison splits. In Granite 4.1 8B, as in Qwen3-8B, the mention moved scores and
decisions more than the superseded assignment. In Gemma 4 12B the superseded assignment moved scores more
($\Delta_{\mathrm{SM}} = +1.513$), with no resolved switch difference. In Qwen3-14B and
Qwen3.5 9B neither difference is resolved; Qwen3.5 9B shows the smallest score shifts and the largest switch
rate. We do not read the differences between models as a trend with size or family. Further Qwen3-14B
estimates, and estimates under the gate rule, are in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

Gemma 3 4B and Ministral 3 14B met no gate cell: current-only decision accuracy was 0.609--0.812 and direct
retrieval 0.916--0.999 (Table~\ref{tab:models-gate}), so no confirmation split was generated. These are
eligibility results, not evidence for or against stale-state interference in these models.
