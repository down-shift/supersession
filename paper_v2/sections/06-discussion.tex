\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} In five models that report both the current and the initial state
correctly, an obsolete state still shifts the preference between routing actions toward the action it
implies. Whether that shift changes the decision depends on the rest of the record. When the other entity's
current state also implies the wrong action, the net switch rate is 0.143--0.233 in histories the models
demonstrably track; in Qwen3-8B, without that cue it is 0.031, and with a reasoning trace it is zero. A
plausible reading is that the obsolete word adds pressure that crosses the decision boundary mostly when a
second cue already pushes toward the same action. Stale-value interference, documented for value queries
\citep{guo2026context,wang2026unable}, thus reaches single-step policy decisions after a single update, on
items where retrieval is at ceiling, but conditionally. The zero switch rate with thinking agrees with
\citet{guo2026context}, whose reasoning models showed no difference between full histories and resolved
snapshots. The interference does not require supersession, nor assignment to the queried attribute: an
incidental mention of the same word shifts preferences in every model, and in Qwen3-8B it changed decisions
even without the second cue (0.177) and, a few times, with thinking. It behaves like entrainment by
decision-relevant words attached to the queried entity \citep{niu2025entrainment}; how it compares with the
superseded assignment differs between models. An unattached word likewise shifted preferences by
2.5--3.0 nats but changed few decisions (0.039 and 0.062): large changes in scored preference need not
change greedy generation.

\paragraph{Consequences for evaluation and mitigation.} Three practical points follow. First, stale-state
benchmarks that vary only update histories cannot tell supersession from incidental mention; a matched
mention condition is cheap and changed our conclusion. Second, decision-level rates depend on what else
the record says, so a benchmark should report which other cues agree with the stale value. Third,
mitigations (which we did not test) should be evaluated the same way, with and without a reasoning step,
and should also check historical retrieval, which a blunt fix could damage.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.} The constructions differ in syntax around the edited word, so the
contrasts compare whole constructions. Correct direct retrieval also does not show that the current state
was the one used at decision time, so the retrieval checks do not rule out stale binding in the sense of
\citet{guo2026context}. The mention construction could be read as more salient. Its
advantage varied with template in Qwen3-8B, was not resolved in three models and reversed in scores in
Gemma 4 12B, which fits a surface
explanation better than a categorical one. Natural state words carry prior meanings, but in Qwen3-8B $D_{\mathrm{S}}$ was positive and
$\Delta_{\mathrm{SM}}$ negative in all 12 task $\times$ cell $\times$ vocabulary strata (Table~\ref{tab:strata}).
