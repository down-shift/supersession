\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} In five models, obsolete states shift routing preferences,
including histories with correct current and initial retrieval. Net switch rates were 0.143--0.233 in
post hoc tracked histories under the main design, where the other entity's state implies the wrong
action. In Qwen3-8B, the superseded rate was 0.031 on balanced histories, against 0.185 on the original
full grid. Different histories and potentially easier equal-state decisions prevent isolating cue
agreement or generalizing that comparison across models. A second cue helping cross the decision
boundary remains a hypothesis.

Our zero superseded-switch result with thinking is consistent with the absence of semantic decision
failures in \citet{guo2026context}'s tested full-history decision conditions, although tasks, models and
comparisons differ. Interference requires neither supersession nor assignment to the queried attribute:
incidental mentions shift preferences in every model, and in Qwen3-8B change decisions in the balanced
run (0.177) and occasionally with thinking. This resembles contextual entrainment
\citep{niu2025entrainment}, but concerns policy-derived action labels rather than direct repetition of
prompt tokens; a shared mechanism is not established. The mention-versus-superseded comparison differs
by model. An unattached word shifted preferences by 2.5--3.0 nats but changed few decisions (0.039 and
0.062), further illustrating that score shifts need not change greedy generation.

\paragraph{Consequences for evaluation and mitigation.} Three practical points follow. First, stale-state
benchmarks that vary only update histories cannot tell supersession from incidental mention; a matched
mention condition is cheap and changed our conclusion. Second, the exploratory comparison motivates reporting and varying which other cues agree with the
stale value; matched histories across models would be needed to isolate their contribution. Third,
mitigations (which we did not test) should be evaluated the same way, with and without a reasoning step,
and should also check historical retrieval, which a blunt fix could damage.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.} The constructions differ in syntax around the edited word, so the
contrasts compare whole constructions. Correct direct retrieval also does not show that the current state
was the one used at decision time, so the retrieval checks do not rule out stale binding in the sense of
\citet{guo2026context}. The mention construction could be read as more salient. Its
advantage varied with template in Qwen3-8B, was not resolved in two models and reversed in scores and switches on gate-selected
cells in Gemma 4 12B, which fits a surface
explanation better than a categorical one. Natural state words carry prior meanings, but in Qwen3-8B $D_{\mathrm{S}}$ was positive and
$\Delta_{\mathrm{SM}}$ negative in all 12 task $\times$ cell $\times$ vocabulary strata (Table~\ref{tab:strata}).
