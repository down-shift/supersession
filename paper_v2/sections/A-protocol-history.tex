\section{Protocol history}
\label{app:history}

Every change below is recorded, with its date, in the protocol documents released with the code. Each
paragraph states what the change preceded. The Qwen3-8B protocol was fixed before its confirmation data
were generated. The second-model and replication amendments were written after Qwen3-8B's confirmation
results, but before any data from the models they added. Two analysis choices were made after
confirmation results: the tracked-history analysis (after Qwen3-8B's) and the confirmation-data rule for
competent cells (after Qwen3-14B's).

\paragraph{v1 (frozen design, never run; audited 2026-10-08).} The design fixed the hypotheses, estimands, factorial,
constructions, gates, bootstrap and held-out confirmation template. An audit before any inference found
two problems. (i) The action events began with a space (\lit{\textvisiblespace APPROVE}). Qwen3-8B begins
its turn without one, and the two strings tokenize differently, so v1 would have scored continuations the
model never produces. (ii) Among the H3 predictors was the decision's own action confidence, which is not
available before the decision is made.

\paragraph{v1b (2026-10-08, before any inference).} Action events became the label exactly as generated plus the
end-of-turn token. The H3 predictors were split into pre-decision predictors and a contemporaneous
reference. Historical retrieval was added as a competence measure. During setup, the runtime was changed
twice for numerical reasons (Appendix~\ref{app:precision}), and a rounding tolerance of $10^{-5}$ was added
to validators that rejected float32 log probabilities a few $10^{-7}$ above zero. The v1b gate failed:
direct retrieval was 0.851--0.946 in every cell. The cause was the prompt format. The user message ended
with a literal \lit{Answer:} line, which the model often echoed (about 260 of 4{,}224 retrieval answers);
about 100 answers gave an action label instead of a state. Only 64 named the obsolete value. The decision
effects replicated on the gate histories.

\paragraph{v2 (2026-10-09, before any v2 data; first redesign, at most two were allowed).} The redesign changed only what the failure
required: it removed the trailing \lit{Answer:} line and used new seeds and entity-name pools so that all
histories were new. Confirmation size was set to four replicates (768 histories) before any v2 data
existed, to fit the compute budget. The scorer was changed from a batched scorer to a key--value-cache
scorer for speed, before any v2 effect was inspected. Both scorers compute the same quantity, and the
cache scorer passed its equivalence check. The v2 gate passed in three cells, and confirmation data were
then generated.

\paragraph{Second model (2026-10-09, after Qwen3-8B's confirmation results, before any Gemma inference).}
The model list, adding Gemma 3 4B with the same rows, rules and runtime, was fixed before any Gemma data
were scored. A 24-record smoke test, inspected for format only, showed that
Gemma writes its label, a newline and then its end-of-turn token. The scored events had omitted the
newline and covered at most a few percent of the probability mass. The scored event was corrected to include the
newline, the checks were rerun, and the smoke outputs were set aside. Development and gate then failed the
competence criteria in every cell (\S\ref{sec:results-gemma}).

\paragraph{Replication models (2026-10-09, after Qwen3-8B's confirmation results, before any inference
for the models listed).} A further amendment listed
five models in a fixed order with pinned revisions. It fixed the per-model sequence, the competence
criteria, the reporting rule, and the definition of competent cells: those passing on the model's own
frozen gate. For this submission the scope was reduced, before any of the five had produced data, to the
first model, Qwen3-14B. The other four are deferred under the same amendment. Qwen3-14B's preflight needed
no correction (causality 0.0; scorer difference $8.3\times10^{-7}$ nats; it ends its answer with the
label and its end-of-turn token). Its rows were byte-identical to Qwen3-8B's in every split.

\paragraph{Models from further families (2026-10-10, after the Qwen3 confirmation results, before any data
from the models listed).} A separate amendment listed Ministral 3 14B, Gemma 4 12B, Qwen3.5 9B, Granite 4.1
8B and OLMo 3 7B, in that order and at pinned revisions, with the same rows, rules, competence criteria and
runtime. A preflight causality check had to give exactly 0.0. It did for the first four; OLMo 3 gave
$2.8\times10^{-5}$ nats and was not run. Two faults stopped the smoke stage and were corrected before any
development data: the format check summed the retrieval candidates' keys instead of their values, and Gemma
4's generation configuration sets two token scores to $-\infty$, which tripped a finiteness check on processed
scores (the check now reads raw logits; decoding and scores are unchanged). Ministral 3 14B met no gate cell
and was not confirmed. The other three produced rows byte-identical to Qwen3-8B's in every split.

\paragraph{Analysis choices after confirmation results.} The tracked-history analysis
(Tables~\ref{tab:joint}, \ref{tab:tracked} and \ref{tab:joint-by-model}) was defined on 2026-10-09, after
Qwen3-8B's confirmation results and before any Qwen3-14B inference, and is post hoc. Its common-set version
(Tables~\ref{tab:tracked} and~\ref{tab:tracked-models}) was added on 2026-10-10, after both models' results. Reporting competent cells by
the confirmation data rather than by each model's frozen gate was decided on 2026-10-10, after Qwen3-14B's
confirmation results. The present manuscript restores the original gate-defined rule for its primary
compact estimates and reports confirmation-selected estimates as a post hoc robustness comparison in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

\paragraph{Never-updated other attribute (2026-10-10, after both models' confirmation results, before any
of its data).} A dated amendment added the fifth construction of Table~\ref{tab:static-other}, its contrast
$\Delta_{\mathrm{ON}}$ and the prediction $\Delta_{\mathrm{ON}} \le 0$, using the sealed routing confirmation
histories of Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B and the same runtime. Before scoring, each model passed the scorer and
causality checks (causality exactly 0.0). As a reproducibility check, 96 records of the original
other-attribute construction were rescored; their action scores matched the sealed ones exactly and all
generations were identical.

\paragraph{Balanced subset (2026-10-10, after the Qwen3 confirmation results, before any of its data).} The
same amendment generated 192 new routing histories (seed 82101, held-out template, a disjoint name pool) in
which the other entity's current state equals the queried entity's, so that it implies the correct action;
everything else follows the confirmation design. Qwen3-8B was scored with the confirmation runtime. The
amendment asked only whether $D_{\mathrm{S}}$ and the superseded net switch rate stay positive; no
test was prespecified. Its causality check names no threshold. Of 24 checks, 23 gave exactly 0.0 and one
gave $5.7\times10^{-5}$ nats, within the runner's tolerance; we report the subset under that rule. The
further-families amendment, by contrast, required exactly 0.0 and excluded OLMo 3 at $2.8\times10^{-5}$.

\paragraph{Thinking mode (2026-10-10, before any thinking-mode data).} A separate amendment generated
answers with thinking enabled for Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B on the sealed routing confirmation rows (superseded,
other-attribute, mention and unattached members, and current-only items; 3,456 items per model), greedy, at
most 4,096 new tokens, with the strict parser applied to the text after the last closing think marker.
Truncated, unclosed or unparseable answers are invalid and count as not wrong. A smoke run fixed the item
set by projected cost (all items for both models). Ministral 3 Reasoning was listed conditional on its
instruction-tuned model passing the gate, which it did not. Qwen3-8B's 2 invalid items had traces that did not
close within the limit; median traces were 525 (Qwen3-8B) and 404 tokens (Qwen3-14B).

\subsection{Numerical causality of log-probability scoring}
\label{app:precision}

Scoring answers by teacher forcing assumes that the log probability at a position does not depend on
tokens appended after it. We tested this directly on the same prompts, by comparing the next-token
distribution at a fixed position with and without one or two tokens appended. With 8-bit weights
(LLM.int8(), \citealp{dettmers2022llmint8}), log probabilities changed by up to 0.843 nats. With bfloat16, the
top-20 log probabilities changed by up to 0.875 nats, and computing only the output layer in float32 reduced it to
0.502. In float32, and with 4-bit nf4 weights, it was exactly zero. In practice this broke our
validators: four disjoint candidate answers summed to more than probability one. A key--value-cache
scorer also disagreed with full recomputation by 3.406 nats under 8-bit weights, but by
$5.08\times10^{-5}$ in float32. Score differences of a few nats are the size of the effects studied here,
so we ran all inference in float32. Score-based studies that use reduced precision should report a
check of this kind.
