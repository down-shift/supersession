\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Conversations and documents often update a fact while leaving its earlier version in view. A user's preference changes, an object moves, a task variable receives a new value. A language model can answer with the current value and still be influenced by the old one: its scores for candidate answers may shift when the old value changes, even though the top answer does not. This paper asks what governs that residual sensitivity.

Consider a history from our main experiment:

\begin{prompt}
Previously, Nora’s badge was amber.
Previously, Liam’s badge was coral.
Currently, Nora’s badge is jade.
Currently, Liam’s badge is pearl.
What is Nora’s current badge?
Respond with only the value, with no explanation.
\end{prompt}

The correct answer is \lit{jade}. Suppose we replace the earlier \lit{amber} with \lit{violet} and leave the other prompt content fixed. The edit can shift the model's scores for \lit{violet} relative to \lit{amber}. If the shift is larger when the question asks about Nora than when it asks about Liam, the score change is query-specific. We call the difference between these two shifts \emph{query relevance}, $R$ (Section~\ref{sec:edits}).

A positive $R$ for the history above could arise for several reasons. The model may represent that \lit{amber} \emph{was} Nora's badge---a superseded assignment to the queried attribute. It may associate \lit{amber} with Nora because they appear in the same sentence, or link them by position because both come first in their blocks. The explicit marker \lit{Previously} could also affect the score contrast. We compare prompt constructions that hold some features fixed while changing others, and cross them with historical and current entity order. The contrasts constrain possible explanations but do not independently identify each factor.

Our results show that query-specific score sensitivity changes across the prompt contrasts we test, but do not decompose it into independent effects of association, relation status, position, and temporal meaning. The order-averaged superseded-minus-other-attribute contrast is 0.314 nats in Qwen and 0.004 in Gemma; Qwen's contrast varies in sign across target attributes. Entity-mention relevance exceeds superseded relevance on average in both models, but the paired aligned-minus-reversed effect for entity mentions points in opposite directions: −1.969 nats [−2.344, −1.604] in Qwen and +1.810 [1.580, 2.058] in Gemma. Unassigned-value relevance reverses sign between order groups in both models. In a separate confirmation, adding the explicit marker \lit{Previously} lowers $R$ in both constructions; its interaction with construction is positive in Qwen and unresolved in Gemma. We interpret these as effects of the tested prompt changes, not evidence for a universal surface-association rule or an isolated obsolete-assignment mechanism.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A counterfactual measure of query-specific sensitivity to a historical value, applied to six prompt constructions crossed with independently varied historical and current mention order (Sections~\ref{sec:task}--\ref{sec:setup}).
\item Controlled contrasts between tested prompt constructions, including attribute-level heterogeneity and model-dependent order effects (Sections~\ref{sec:main-findings}--\ref{sec:marker-results}).
\item Order-stratified results: never-assigned-value relevance has opposite signs across aligned and reversed mention order, and averages near zero when the order groups are combined (Section~\ref{sec:order}).
\item A separately specified test of adding the explicit marker \lit{Previously}, which lowers relevance in both tested constructions and models; its effect on their construction gap differs by model (Section~\ref{sec:marker-results}).
\end{enumerate}
