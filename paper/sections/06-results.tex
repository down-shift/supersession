\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Effects of tested prompt contrasts}
\label{sec:main-findings}

Figure~\ref{fig:construction-order} displays $R$ directly by construction and order for Experiment 1; Figure~\ref{fig:marker-effects} reports the separate marker experiment. The order-averaged superseded-minus-other-attribute contrast is 0.314 nats (95\% CI [0.147, 0.482]) in Qwen and 0.004 [−0.104, 0.118] in Gemma. Qwen's contrast varies in sign across attributes: it is positive for badge and color, negative for code, and unresolved for label; Gemma's attribute-specific intervals include zero (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-other}). This average is smaller than the entity-mention-minus-superseded contrast, but does not show that relation status has a consistently small effect. Entity-mention relevance exceeds superseded relevance by 3.504 [3.198, 3.806] and 2.589 [2.361, 2.829] nats in Qwen and Gemma. For unassigned values, aligned-minus-reversed relevance is 6.578 [6.356, 6.789] nats for early unassigned in Qwen and 4.970 [4.700, 5.257] in Gemma; it is also large for late unassigned. Superseded relevance stays positive in both order groups and has a smaller paired order effect. Entity-mention relevance also stays positive, but its order effect points in opposite directions across models: aligned-minus-reversed is −1.969 [−2.344, −1.604] nats in Qwen and +1.810 [1.580, 2.058] in Gemma. The estimates describe the prompt contrasts tested and do not isolate a single mechanism.

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives $R$ for every construction. The live control shows the scale of a fully relevant edit: 32.598 nats in Qwen and 39.355 in Gemma. Superseded and other-attribute relevance are positive and similar to each other in both order groups. Entity-mention relevance is the largest among the non-live constructions. Unassigned relevance is near zero overall but large and of opposite sign in the two order groups.

These score changes coexist with correct candidate rankings. In the superseded construction both models ranked the current value strictly first in all 1,536 baseline and all 1,536 edited prompts, with mean current-minus-earlier margins of 21.734 nats (Qwen) and 23.854 (Gemma). The edited prompts changed the source and donor log scores, both of which had low absolute mass: Qwen's mean superseded source score fell from −22.271 to −23.881, while the donor score rose from −23.618 to −22.120 (Appendix~\ref{app:score-changes}). These changes do not imply conservation of probability between the two candidates or a corresponding change in generated-answer probability.

\subsection{Relation-status and construction contrasts}
\label{sec:relation}

The superseded and other-attribute constructions share a sentence frame, marker, position, and entity--value pairing. They differ in the earlier attribute word and whether the current block updates that attribute (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition-logic}); this contrast does not separate these two changes. The average superseded-minus-other-attribute contrast is 0.314 nats in Qwen and 0.004 in Gemma (Table~\ref{tab:frozen}). Qwen's attribute-specific estimates range from −0.570 for \lit{code} to 0.876 for \lit{color}, with badge 0.748 [0.512, 0.969] and label 0.200 [−0.042, 0.459]. The average therefore masks meaningful heterogeneity. Gemma's attribute-specific estimates are near zero with intervals spanning zero (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-other}); this is not an equivalence test.

Mentioning the earlier value in a note about the entity, without assigning it, produces more relevance than the superseded assignment: superseded minus entity mention is −3.504 nats in Qwen and −2.589 in Gemma. This holds for every target attribute in both models (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-entity}). The two constructions differ in predicate, syntax, and the presence of \lit{Previously}; Experiment 2 removes the last of these differences (Section~\ref{sec:marker-results}).

These three contrasts were the test of our original hypothesis, which predicted that superseded relevance would exceed all three controls, each with a lower 95\% bound above zero, both overall and under reversed order. The prediction fails in both models, because the entity-mention contrast is negative overall and under reversed order (Table~\ref{tab:b-order}).

\subsection{Relative position}
\label{sec:order}

For unassigned values, aligned order places the first value in the same ordinal slot as the first-listed entity in the current block; reversing the order changes which entity occupies that slot. Relevance changes sign across these order groups for early and late unassigned values in both models (Table~\ref{tab:main}). This behavior is consistent with ordinal position linking otherwise unattached values and entities, but does not identify the internal mechanism. Superseded relevance remains positive in both order groups (2.144 aligned and 1.804 reversed in Qwen; 1.703 and 1.672 in Gemma), with smaller paired order effects than the unassigned contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:b-paired-r}). Entity-mention relevance also remains positive in both groups, although its magnitude is order-dependent and the direction of its paired effect differs by model (Qwen −1.970; Gemma +1.809 nats, aligned minus reversed). Explicit entity association thus preserves positive relevance across the two order groups in these prompts; it does not make order irrelevant.

Averaging over orders hides the unassigned-value pattern. Overall unassigned relevance is near zero (−0.037 and 0.001 in Qwen; 0.113 and 0.125 in Gemma) not because the edit is ignored but because aligned and reversed effects cancel. The edit to an early unassigned value shifts scores under both queries---by 8.202 and 8.239 nats in Qwen and by 6.347 and 6.234 in Gemma---with little query specificity once orders are pooled. Superseded-minus-unassigned contrasts are negative under aligned order and positive under reversed order in both models; the contrast plot is retained as an appendix diagnostic (Figure~\ref{fig:order}). The positive order-averaged contrast of the preliminary comparison (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}) is consistent with this pattern, although that comparison used different templates.

\subsection{The explicit marker}
\label{sec:marker-results}

Experiment 2 tests adding the explicit prefix \lit{Previously,} to each historical line; the marker-absent superseded sentence still uses past tense (Table~\ref{tab:marker-templates}). Adding the marker lowers $R$ in both constructions and models, with every within-construction interval below zero (Table~\ref{tab:marker}). The order-averaged paired entity-mention-minus-superseded construction gap remains positive without the marker: 3.565 nats (95\% CI [2.806, 4.286]) in Qwen and 0.683 [0.435, 0.951] in Gemma. The gap is order-dependent: in Gemma's marker-absent reversed-order cell, entity-mention $R$ is 2.275 and superseded $R$ is 2.445. In Qwen, the marker-by-construction interaction is 1.337 nats [0.976, 1.712], positive in both order groups; in Gemma it is −0.125 [−0.390, 0.136], unresolved. These are effects of adding the explicit marker on the measured query-specific score contrast; they do not show active suppression or isolate temporal meaning from the inserted tokens.

An exploratory Phi-4-mini scoring run on the same dataset showed a different interaction pattern: adding the marker reduced superseded $R$ by 1.355 nats [−1.561, −1.138], while the entity-mention change was 0.145 [−0.018, 0.310], and the paired interaction was −1.500 [−1.734, −1.264] (Appendix~\ref{app:marker}, Table~\ref{tab:d-phi-marker}). Because this model extension was exploratory and used candidate scoring without generated answers, it is supplementary evidence about score sensitivity, not an answer-accuracy result or a generalization across models.
