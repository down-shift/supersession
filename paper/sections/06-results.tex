\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What changes query relevance}
\label{sec:main-findings}

Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} sets the comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:decomposition-logic} side by side for Qwen and Gemma. Relation status matters little on average: superseded relevance exceeds other-attribute relevance by 0.314 nats (95\% CI [0.147, 0.482]) in Qwen and by 0.004 [−0.104, 0.118] in Gemma. The figure adds two comparisons from a separate update design (Section~\ref{sec:relation-split}). Construction matters more, with entity-mention relevance above superseded relevance by 3.504 [3.198, 3.806] and 2.589 [2.361, 2.829] nats, and a smaller gap remains with the marker and name--value distance matched (Section~\ref{sec:entity-gap}). The rows of Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} are comparisons from different designs and stimulus sets, not a factorial decomposition, so they do not add up to one another. Mention order decides the sign for never-assigned values. Reversing the order lowers early-unassigned relevance by 6.578 [6.356, 6.789] nats in Qwen and 4.970 [4.700, 5.257] in Gemma, taking it from positive to negative, while the same reversal moves superseded relevance by only 0.340 [0.218, 0.470] and 0.032 [−0.101, 0.168]. For entity mentions the aligned-minus-reversed difference has opposite signs in the two models: −1.969 [−2.344, −1.604] in Qwen and +1.810 [1.580, 2.058] in Gemma. In Experiment 2, adding the prefix \lit{Previously,} lowers relevance in the tested constructions by between 0.652 and 1.734 nats in each construction and model.

These score changes coexist with correct rankings. In the superseded construction both models ranked the current value strictly first in all 1,536 baseline and all 1,536 edited prompts, with mean current-minus-earlier margins of 21.734 nats (Qwen) and 23.854 (Gemma). The edited source and donor candidates both had low absolute log masses: in Qwen's superseded construction the source fell from −22.271 to −23.881 nats on average and the donor rose from −23.618 to −22.120, against a current candidate near zero (Table~\ref{tab:b-abs-mass}), so their changes do not imply that probability moves from one to the other, or a matching change in generated answers.

\subsection{Relation status and construction}
\label{sec:relation}

Superseded minus other attribute is the study's closest estimate of what being an obsolete value of the queried attribute adds (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition-logic} explains why it joins two properties). In Qwen the 0.314-nat average, about a sixth of superseded relevance, masks heterogeneity across target attributes, from −0.570 for \lit{code} to 0.876 for \lit{color} (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-other}); Gemma's attribute-specific intervals all include zero, which is not evidence of equivalence. Mentioning the earlier value in a note about the entity, without assigning it, produces more relevance than the superseded assignment in both models and for every target attribute (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-entity}). These contrasts, with superseded minus early unassigned (Table~\ref{tab:frozen}), were the test of our original hypothesis, which predicted that superseded relevance would exceed all three controls, each with a lower 95\% bound above zero, both overall and under reversed order. The prediction fails in both models, because the entity-mention contrast is negative overall and under reversed order (Table~\ref{tab:b-order}).

\subsection{Mention order}
\label{sec:order}

When no sentence links a value to an entity, the models link them by position. Under aligned order the first unassigned value sits in the same slot as the entity listed first in the current block, and an edit to it is more relevant to that entity's query; under reversed order the same value lines up with the other entity, and relevance turns negative. The pattern holds for early and late unassigned mentions in both models, with paired order effects between 4.970 and 6.639 nats and positive in every one of the 96 histories (Table~\ref{tab:b-paired-r}). We say that such a value is \emph{credited} to an entity as shorthand for this query-specific score contrast, which follows matching ordinal positions across the historical and current blocks. It is distinct from primacy or recency: the unassigned lines occupy the same positions in both order groups, and early and late placement show the same sign pattern. This behavior is consistent with slot-based matching of values to entities in these prompts. Related mechanistic work identifies abstract binding IDs \citep{feng2024bind} and ordering IDs that track entities by their order of introduction \citep{prakash2026lookbacks}. None of this identifies the mechanism here.

Averaging over orders hides this pattern: overall unassigned relevance is near zero (−0.037 and 0.001 in Qwen; 0.113 and 0.125 in Gemma) not because the edit is ignored (an early unassigned edit shifts scores by 6.234 to 8.239 nats under either query in both models) but because aligned and reversed effects cancel. Explicit association keeps relevance positive in both order groups: superseded relevance changes little with order, while entity-mention relevance changes in opposite directions in the two models (Figure~\ref{fig:order}, Appendix~\ref{app:exp1}).

\subsection{The explicit marker}
\label{sec:marker-results}

Experiment 2 adds or removes the prefix \lit{Previously,} with the rest of each prompt fixed (Table~\ref{tab:marker-templates}). Adding the prefix \lit{Previously,} lowers $R$ in the tested constructions in both models, with every within-construction interval below zero (Table~\ref{tab:marker}). In Qwen the reduction is larger for entity mentions: the marker-by-construction interaction is 1.082 [0.850, 1.308], positive in both order groups. In Gemma the pooled interaction, 0.125 [−0.012, 0.265], is unresolved because the two order groups have opposing, resolved interactions: 0.482 [0.307, 0.655] under aligned and $-0.232$ [$-0.436$, $-0.027$] under reversed order, a paired aligned-minus-reversed difference of 0.715 [0.461, 0.968] (in Qwen $-0.896$ [$-1.191$, $-0.577$]; Figure~\ref{fig:marker-effects}). The earlier 24-history run also found negative marker effects, a positive pooled interaction in Qwen, and an unresolved pooled interaction in Gemma; its order-specific Gemma intervals include zero, so it did not establish the crossover (Appendix~\ref{app:marker}). Relevance stays positive with the marker in every construction and order group, so adding the prefix lowers query-specific sensitivity to the earlier value without removing it. Without the marker, entity-mention relevance still exceeds superseded relevance, by 3.059 [2.758, 3.370] nats in Qwen and 0.908 [0.783, 1.034] in Gemma, although in Gemma's reversed-order cell the difference is not resolved (−0.175 [−0.357, 0.011]). An exploratory Phi-4-mini run showed a third pattern (Appendix~\ref{app:marker}).

\subsection{Separating relation status}
\label{sec:relation-split}

The updated-other-attribute follow-up varies whether the other attribute is also updated. With prompt length and layout matched, having been the queried attribute raised relevance (superseded minus updated other attribute: 0.740 [0.661, 0.818] nats in Qwen, 0.148 [0.038, 0.261] in Gemma), while being contradicted by an update lowered it in Qwen (updated minus static other attribute: −0.289 [−0.363, −0.215]) but not detectably in Gemma (−0.028 [−0.137, 0.076]; Table~\ref{tab:update}). In this design Gemma ranks the current candidate first on only 2,895 of 3,072 superseded prompts, below the 99\% screening level (Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}). Restricting to histories in which every prompt is ranked correctly preserves the positive queried-attribute effects and Qwen's negative update effect; Gemma's update effect remains unresolved: 0.734 [0.662, 0.811] and $-0.276$ [$-0.353$, $-0.200$] in Qwen (87 histories), 0.277 [0.080, 0.481] and an unresolved 0.047 [$-0.123$, 0.221] in Gemma, although only 28 of Gemma's 96 histories qualify. Both contrasts are conditional: the first compares queried-attribute status when both assignments are updated, the second compares updating when the assignment concerns another attribute. In Qwen the result supports an account in which a positive attribute effect and a negative update effect partly cancel; the follow-up uses different prompts and does not decompose the Experiment 1 estimate. The recorded prediction of the discount account holds for Qwen and is unresolved for Gemma.

\subsection{Why entity mentions outweigh assignments}
\label{sec:entity-gap}

Matching the name--value token gap does not remove the construction gap in the tested templates. Moving the name next to the value raised superseded relevance in both models (near minus far: 0.500 [0.400, 0.599] in Qwen, 0.393 [0.288, 0.496] in Gemma) but not entity-mention relevance (−0.370 [−0.498, −0.249] and −0.027 [−0.110, 0.058]; Table~\ref{tab:distance}), so the recorded prediction, near above far in both constructions, fails in both models. Table~\ref{tab:gap} lists the gap in each tested combination of marker placement and name--value distance; it is smallest, 1.293 and 0.367 nats, with the marker on both lines and a one-token name--value distance in both. The rows come from different stimulus sets, so they are matched comparisons rather than an additive decomposition, and the distance rewrites also change syntax; the far conditions are not matched either (seven tokens for entity mention against three or four for superseded), so this is not a factorial distance manipulation. Predicate and framing are the remaining candidate explanations, alongside other wording differences.

\subsection{Below ceiling}
\label{sec:below-ceiling}

In the exploratory study of OLMo-2-7B-Instruct, which fails the screen and answers below ceiling, generated answers were correct for 2,950 of 3,072 superseded prompts but only 1,846 of 3,072 entity-mention prompts and 1,528 and 2,131 of the early- and late-unassigned prompts (Table~\ref{tab:olmo}); most errors name one of the two earlier values or no valid value (Table~\ref{tab:f-olmo-errors}). Donor following, a separate descriptive measure on edited prompts only, shows that editing a value mentioned with the queried entity moved answers to the donor query-specifically (0.155 [0.124, 0.190]); never-assigned values pulled answers toward the entity in the matching position (early unassigned: 0.161 [0.122, 0.203] aligned, −0.164 [−0.214, −0.120] reversed); superseded values almost never surfaced, although the superseded edit still moved answers by a small, query-specific amount (0.008 [0.003, 0.014]). A baseline-adjusted generation contrast gives the same ordering, 0.320 [0.268, 0.379] for entity mention against 0.017 [0.007, 0.029] for superseded (Table~\ref{tab:f-olmo-r}).

OLMo-2 is separate behavioral evidence from below ceiling. Its generated answers follow the construction and position ordering of most screened models' scores: mentioned and position-aligned values pull answers toward the donor, and superseded values rarely do. Its own candidate-score $R$ does not: 6.157 [5.933, 6.393] nats for superseded against 5.350 [5.040, 5.669] for entity mention. In this model, then, $R$ does not predict generated-answer behavior, and OLMo-2's candidate-score ordering differs from its generated-answer ordering.

\subsection{Depth of updates}
\label{sec:depth}

Chain depth is the total number of successive assignments of the queried attribute, including the current one, so depth 3 means two updates. Two separate runs address depth. In a development run, both models generated the correct answer for every prompt at depths 3, 4, and 5, so by the recorded rule no generation confirmation was run (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}). An independent score-only run on 96 fresh histories per depth then estimated how $R$ changes with depth; it collected no generated answers, and its current-candidate rank accuracy, which is not generated-answer accuracy, was at least 3,067 of 3,072 at every depth in both models (Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}). In that run Qwen's $R$ for the edited immediately superseded value rose from 7.353 [6.972, 7.725] nats at depth 3 to 10.762 [10.303, 11.220] at depth 4 and 11.835 [11.424, 12.286] at depth 5; the depth-5-minus-depth-3 difference of 4.482 [3.915, 5.047] meets the recorded criterion. Gemma's $R$ stayed between 3.450 and 3.708 (depth 5 minus depth 3: 0.258 [−0.063, 0.557]). Deeper chains are also longer and add preceding text, so the growth need not come from the number of updates as such; the interference that \citet{wang2025unable} observe in answers as updates accumulate is an analogy, not an identified cause.

\subsection{Additional models}
\label{sec:additional-models}

Of seven additional models, five passed the competence screen: Falcon3-7B, Granite-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma 3 12B and Qwen3-14B; OLMo-2-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct failed (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}). In Table~\ref{tab:models}, relation status ranges from $-1.305$ [$-2.048$, $-0.648$] nats in Mistral-7B through no resolvable effect in Falcon3-7B to 1.162 [0.988, 1.351] in Qwen3-14B, about a quarter of its superseded relevance. Within each family the larger model weights relation status more and the entity-mention gap less (Qwen3-8B to 14B: 0.314 to 1.162 and 3.504 to 2.380; Gemma 3 4B to 12B: 0.004 to 0.442 and 2.589 to 1.321); with two sizes per family this is an observation, not a trend. In Granite-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B the entity-mention gap does not resolvably exceed relation status (paired differences 0.371 [$-0.019$, 0.744] and 0.889 [$-0.134$, 1.986]), and these two models have the largest positional effects. Mistral-7B's entity-mention gap changes sign with mention order (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}). Although it passed the screen, Mistral-7B ranks the current candidate first on only 3,020 of 3,072 other-attribute confirmation prompts (98.3\%; Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}). On the 68 histories in which every prompt is ranked correctly, its relation-status contrast is $-0.037$ [$-0.680$, 0.541], while its unresolved entity-mention gap and its positional effect remain; its negative relation status therefore depends on histories with ranking errors.

The threshold excludes two models narrowly, Phi-4-mini and Qwen2.5-7B, each below 99\% in one construction. Scored descriptively, both meet the two criteria recorded before scoring: the entity-mention gap exceeds the relation-status contrast in absolute value, with (b) $-$ (a) and (b) $+$ (a) both above zero, and early-unassigned relevance is positive under aligned and negative under reversed order (Table~\ref{tab:f-excluded}). In both, superseded relevance falls below other-attribute relevance.
