\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What changes query relevance}
\label{sec:main-findings}

Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} sets the comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:decomposition-logic} side by side for Qwen and Gemma. Relation status matters little on average: superseded relevance exceeds other-attribute relevance by 0.314 nats (95\% CI [0.147, 0.482]) in Qwen and by 0.004 [−0.104, 0.118] in Gemma. A separate follow-up design with different prompts (Section~\ref{sec:relation-split}) compares superseded with updated other-attribute values (0.740 [0.661, 0.818] nats in Qwen, 0.148 [0.038, 0.261] in Gemma) and updated with static other-attribute values ($-0.289$ [$-0.363$, $-0.215$] and an unresolved $-0.028$ [$-0.137$, 0.076]). Construction matters more, with entity-mention relevance above superseded relevance by 3.504 [3.198, 3.806] and 2.589 [2.361, 2.829] nats; with the marker and name--value distance matched, the gap shrinks to 1.293 [1.019, 1.571] and 0.367 [0.234, 0.514] but remains (Section~\ref{sec:entity-gap}). The rows of Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} are comparisons from different designs and stimulus sets, not a factorial decomposition, so they do not add up to one another. Mention order decides the sign for never-assigned values. Reversing the order lowers early-unassigned relevance by 6.578 [6.356, 6.789] nats in Qwen and 4.970 [4.700, 5.257] in Gemma, taking it from positive to negative, while the same reversal moves superseded relevance by only 0.340 [0.218, 0.470] and 0.032 [−0.101, 0.168]. For entity mentions the aligned-minus-reversed difference has opposite signs in the two models: −1.969 [−2.344, −1.604] in Qwen and +1.810 [1.580, 2.058] in Gemma. In Experiment 2, adding \lit{Previously} lowers relevance by between 0.652 and 1.734 nats in each construction and model.

Table~\ref{tab:models} gives the main comparisons for all seven models that passed the competence screen. In every one, reversing mention order moves early-unassigned relevance from positive to negative, by more than relation status moves relevance in any model. Entity mentions outweigh superseded assignments in all but Mistral-7B, and the paired difference between that gap and relation status is resolved in all but Granite-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B (Section~\ref{sec:additional-models}). $R$ for every construction and order group is in Appendix~\ref{app:exp1} (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:construction-order}); the live control, whose edit changes the correct answer, gives 32.598 nats in Qwen and 39.355 in Gemma.

These score changes coexist with correct rankings. In the superseded construction both models ranked the current value strictly first in all 1,536 baseline and all 1,536 edited prompts, with mean current-minus-earlier margins of 21.734 nats (Qwen) and 23.854 (Gemma). The edited source and donor candidates both had low absolute log masses: in Qwen's superseded construction the source fell from −22.271 to −23.881 nats on average and the donor rose from −23.618 to −22.120, against a current candidate near zero (Table~\ref{tab:b-abs-mass}), so their changes do not imply that probability moves from one to the other, or a matching change in generated answers.

\subsection{Relation status and construction}
\label{sec:relation}

Superseded minus other attribute is the study's closest estimate of what being an obsolete value of the queried attribute adds (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition-logic} explains why it joins two properties). In Qwen the 0.314-nat average, about a sixth of superseded relevance, masks heterogeneity across target attributes, from −0.570 for \lit{code} to 0.876 for \lit{color} (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-other}); Gemma's attribute-specific intervals all include zero, which is not evidence of equivalence. Mentioning the earlier value in a note about the entity, without assigning it, produces more relevance than the superseded assignment in both models and for every target attribute (Table~\ref{tab:b-attr-entity}). These contrasts, with superseded minus early unassigned (Table~\ref{tab:frozen}), were the test of our original hypothesis, which predicted that superseded relevance would exceed all three controls, each with a lower 95\% bound above zero, both overall and under reversed order. The prediction fails in both models, because the entity-mention contrast is negative overall and under reversed order (Table~\ref{tab:b-order}).

\subsection{Mention order}
\label{sec:order}

When no sentence links a value to an entity, the models link them by position. Under aligned order the first unassigned value sits in the same slot as the entity listed first in the current block, and an edit to it is more relevant to that entity's query; under reversed order the same value lines up with the other entity, and relevance turns negative. The pattern holds for early and late unassigned mentions in both models, with paired order effects between 4.970 and 6.639 nats and positive in every one of the 96 histories (Table~\ref{tab:b-paired-r}). This behavior is consistent with slot-based matching of values to entities in these prompts. It is related to the ordinal structure that \citet{feng2024bind} report in binding IDs and to the ordering IDs of \citet{prakash2026lookbacks}, which identify an item as first or second in its block; \citet{gurarieh2026mixing} find that positional retrieval degrades as bindings multiply. None of this identifies the mechanism here.

Averaging over orders hides this pattern: overall unassigned relevance is near zero (−0.037 and 0.001 in Qwen; 0.113 and 0.125 in Gemma) not because the edit is ignored (an early unassigned edit shifts scores by 8.202 and 8.239 nats under the two queries in Qwen, and by 6.347 and 6.234 in Gemma) but because aligned and reversed effects cancel. Explicit association keeps relevance positive in both order groups: superseded relevance changes little with order, while entity-mention relevance changes in opposite directions in the two models (Figure~\ref{fig:order}, Appendix~\ref{app:exp1}).

\subsection{The explicit marker}
\label{sec:marker-results}

Experiment 2 adds or removes the prefix \lit{Previously,} with the rest of each prompt fixed (Table~\ref{tab:marker-templates}). Adding the marker lowers $R$ in both constructions and both models, with every within-construction interval below zero (Table~\ref{tab:marker}). In Qwen the reduction is larger for entity mentions: the marker-by-construction interaction is 1.082 [0.850, 1.308], positive in both order groups. In Gemma the pooled interaction, 0.125 [−0.012, 0.265], is unresolved because the two order groups have opposing, resolved interactions: 0.482 [0.307, 0.655] under aligned and $-0.232$ [$-0.436$, $-0.027$] under reversed order, a paired aligned-minus-reversed difference of 0.715 [0.461, 0.968] (in Qwen $-0.896$ [$-1.191$, $-0.577$]; Figure~\ref{fig:marker-effects}). An earlier 24-history run gave the same pattern (Appendix~\ref{app:marker}). Relevance stays positive with the marker in every construction and order group, so the marker lowers query-specific sensitivity to the earlier value without removing it; a lower score contrast is not evidence of active suppression. Without the marker, entity-mention relevance still exceeds superseded relevance, by 3.059 [2.758, 3.370] nats in Qwen and 0.908 [0.783, 1.034] in Gemma, although in Gemma's reversed-order cell the difference is not resolved (−0.175 [−0.357, 0.011]). An exploratory Phi-4-mini run on the 24-history dataset showed yet another pattern, with the marker lowering superseded relevance (−1.355 [−1.561, −1.138]) but not entity-mention relevance (0.145 [−0.018, 0.310]).

\subsection{Separating relation status}
\label{sec:relation-split}

The updated-other-attribute follow-up separates the two properties joined in the relation-status contrast. With prompt length and layout matched, having been the queried attribute raised relevance (superseded minus updated other attribute: 0.740 [0.661, 0.818] nats in Qwen, 0.148 [0.038, 0.261] in Gemma), while being contradicted by an update lowered it in Qwen (updated minus static other attribute: −0.289 [−0.363, −0.215]) but not detectably in Gemma (−0.028 [−0.137, 0.076]; Table~\ref{tab:update}). In this design Gemma ranks the current candidate first on only 2,895 of 3,072 superseded prompts, below the 99\% screening level (Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}). Both contrasts are conditional: the first compares queried-attribute status when both assignments are updated, the second compares updating when the assignment concerns another attribute. In Qwen the result supports an account in which a positive attribute effect and a negative update effect partly cancel; the follow-up uses different prompts and does not decompose the Experiment 1 estimate. The recorded prediction of the discount account holds for Qwen and is unresolved for Gemma.

\subsection{Why entity mentions outweigh assignments}
\label{sec:entity-gap}

Matching the name--value token gap does not remove the construction gap in the tested templates. Moving the name next to the value raised superseded relevance in both models (near minus far: 0.500 [0.400, 0.599] in Qwen, 0.393 [0.288, 0.496] in Gemma) but not entity-mention relevance (−0.370 [−0.498, −0.249] and −0.027 [−0.110, 0.058]; Table~\ref{tab:distance}), so the recorded prediction, near above far in both constructions, fails in both models. Table~\ref{tab:gap} lists the gap in each tested combination of marker placement and name--value distance: 3.504 and 2.589 nats in Experiment 1, where only the superseded line carries \lit{Previously} and its value sits three or four tokens after the name; 1.976 and 0.782 in Experiment 2 with the marker on both lines; and 1.293 and 0.367 in the distance follow-up with the marker on both lines and a one-token name--value distance in both. The rows come from different stimulus sets, so they are matched comparisons rather than an additive decomposition, and the distance rewrites also change syntax; the far conditions are not matched either (seven tokens for entity mention against three or four for superseded), so this is not a factorial distance manipulation. Predicate and framing are the remaining candidate explanations, alongside other wording differences.

\subsection{Below ceiling}
\label{sec:below-ceiling}

In the exploratory study of OLMo-2-7B-Instruct, which fails the screen and answers below ceiling, generated answers were correct for 2,950 of 3,072 superseded prompts but only 1,846 of 3,072 entity-mention prompts and 1,528 and 2,131 of the early- and late-unassigned prompts (Table~\ref{tab:olmo}); most errors name one of the two earlier values or no valid value (Table~\ref{tab:f-olmo-errors}). Donor following, a separate descriptive measure on edited prompts only, shows that editing a value mentioned with the queried entity moved answers to the donor query-specifically (0.155 [0.124, 0.190]); never-assigned values pulled answers toward the entity in the matching position (early unassigned: 0.161 [0.122, 0.203] aligned, −0.164 [−0.214, −0.120] reversed); superseded values almost never surfaced, although the superseded edit still moved answers by a small, query-specific amount (0.008 [0.003, 0.014]). A baseline-adjusted generation contrast gives the same ordering, 0.320 [0.268, 0.379] for entity mention against 0.017 [0.007, 0.029] for superseded (Table~\ref{tab:f-olmo-r}). OLMo-2's candidate scores do not follow that ordering: its score-based $R$ is 6.157 [5.933, 6.393] nats for superseded against 5.350 [5.040, 5.669] for entity mention, unlike every screened model.

\subsection{Depth of updates}
\label{sec:depth}

Chain depth is the total number of successive assignments of the queried attribute, including the current one, so depth 3 means two updates. Two separate runs address depth. In a development run (12 histories per depth, 384 generated answers per depth and model), both models answered every prompt correctly at depths 3, 4, and 5, so by the recorded rule no generation confirmation was run. An independent score-only run on 96 fresh histories per depth then estimated how $R$ changes with depth; it collected no generated answers, and its current-candidate rank accuracy, which is not generated-answer accuracy, was 3,072 of 3,072 prompts at every depth in Qwen and 3,071, 3,067, and 3,070 of 3,072 at depths 3, 4, and 5 in Gemma (Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}). In that run Qwen's $R$ for the edited immediately superseded value rose from 7.353 [6.972, 7.725] nats at depth 3 to 10.762 [10.303, 11.220] at depth 4 and 11.835 [11.424, 12.286] at depth 5; the depth-5-minus-depth-3 difference of 4.482 [3.915, 5.047] meets the recorded criterion. Gemma's $R$ stayed between 3.450 and 3.708 (depth 5 minus depth 3: 0.258 [−0.063, 0.557]). Deeper chains are also longer and add preceding text, so the growth need not come from the number of updates as such; the interference that \citet{wang2025unable} observe in answers as updates accumulate is an analogy, not an identified cause.

\subsection{Additional models}
\label{sec:additional-models}

Of seven additional models, five passed the competence screen: Falcon3-7B, Granite-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma 3 12B and Qwen3-14B. OLMo-2-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct failed (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}). In Table~\ref{tab:models}, relation status ranges from $-1.305$ [$-2.048$, $-0.648$] nats in Mistral-7B, where superseded values count less than other-attribute values, through no resolvable effect in Falcon3-7B ($-0.052$ [$-0.168$, 0.058]), to 1.162 [0.988, 1.351] in Qwen3-14B, about a quarter of its superseded relevance. Within each family the larger model weights relation status more and the entity-mention gap less (Qwen3-8B to Qwen3-14B: 0.314 to 1.162 and 3.504 to 2.380; Gemma 3 4B to 12B: 0.004 to 0.442 and 2.589 to 1.321); with two sizes per family this is an observation, not a trend. Granite-3.1-8B is the one screened model in which the entity-mention gap does not resolvably exceed relation status (paired difference 0.371 [$-0.019$, 0.744]); position matters far more there than either, moving early-unassigned relevance from 5.105 to $-5.616$. Mistral-7B's entity-mention gap is unresolved overall ($-0.415$ [$-1.000$, 0.187]) because it changes sign with mention order: superseded relevance exceeds entity-mention relevance by 2.417 [1.596, 3.224] under aligned order, and entity-mention relevance exceeds superseded relevance by 1.586 [0.969, 2.242] under reversed order. Its positional effect on never-assigned values is the largest of any model (early unassigned 5.975 aligned, $-5.862$ reversed). Although it passed the screen, Mistral-7B ranks the current candidate first on only 3,020 of 3,072 other-attribute confirmation prompts (98.3\%; Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}). Restricted, as in Table~\ref{tab:b-selected}, to the 68 histories in which every prompt is ranked correctly, its relation-status contrast is $-0.037$ [$-0.680$, 0.541] and its entity-mention gap $-0.447$ [$-1.039$, 0.185], while the positional effect remains (early unassigned 5.359 aligned, $-4.980$ reversed). Its negative relation status therefore depends on histories with ranking errors; its unresolved construction difference and its positional effect do not.

The threshold excludes two models narrowly, Phi-4-mini and Qwen2.5-7B, each below 99\% in a single construction (Mistral-7B, which had narrowly failed an earlier version of the screen, passes this one). Scored descriptively, both meet the two criteria recorded before scoring. The entity-mention gap (b: 1.872 and 2.253 nats) exceeds the relation-status contrast (a: $-0.152$ and $-0.553$) in absolute value: paired within history, (b) $-$ (a) is 2.025 [1.759, 2.306] and 2.806 [2.432, 3.227], and (b) $+$ (a) is 1.720 [1.527, 1.911] and 1.700 [1.357, 2.043], all lower bounds above zero. Early-unassigned relevance is positive under aligned and negative under reversed order (Table~\ref{tab:f-excluded}). In both, superseded relevance falls below other-attribute relevance. The two models narrowly excluded by the threshold show the same ordering.
