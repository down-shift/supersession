\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

A model's sensitivity to an earlier value tracks how that value is attached to an entity in the text more than what the value used to mean. A value that was the queried attribute and has since been updated, and a value that was some other attribute of the same entity and was never updated, differ on average by about a third of a nat or less in Qwen3-8B and Gemma 3 4B and by $-1.305$ to 1.162 nats in the other screened models, although only the first is contradicted by the current state. In six of the seven models, a value placed in a sentence with the entity, even a note described as unrelated, produces more query-specific sensitivity than a superseded assignment, in Qwen and Gemma also with the marker and name--value distance matched (Table~\ref{tab:gap}), and a value attached to no one is credited to whichever entity shares its position. For the question that motivated this work, whether models retain obsolete assignments, these scores say that the residual sensitivity is not specific to obsolescence, at least in the averages and directions the models share (Table~\ref{tab:models}). Beyond position, the ranking has exceptions. In Granite-3.1-8B the entity-mention gap and relation status are of similar size. In Mistral-7B the construction difference changes sign with mention order, and superseded values count less than other-attribute values. An update discount that outweighs the attribute effect, as the update follow-up found in part for Qwen, would produce that pattern, but it is untested in Mistral and the negative estimate disappears once histories with ranking errors are excluded. In the larger Qwen and Gemma models relation status is resolved and positive. Mistral-7B and Granite-3.1-8B also have the largest positional effects. The failure of the prespecified relation-selective hypothesis does not mean relation status has no effect: in a follow-up with different prompts, Qwen shows a positive attribute effect and a negative update effect, consistent with the two partly cancelling, though the follow-up does not decompose the Experiment 1 estimate.

The shared regularities sit alongside model-specific ones. Both models credit unattached values by position, and both give entity mentions more weight than superseded assignments on average. But reversing mention order raises entity-mention relevance in Qwen and lowers it in Gemma; the marker reduces entity-mention relevance more than superseded relevance in Qwen in both order groups, while in Gemma that interaction reverses sign between order groups; and in Gemma's reversed-order prompts without the marker, the difference between entity mentions and superseded assignments is not resolved. An exploratory Phi-4-mini run showed a third marker pattern. The data therefore support a claim about which prompt features carry weight, not a single rule that predicts their direction in every model. The positional result connects these short prompts to findings in longer ones: \citet{liu2024lost} and \citet{yang2026conflicts} report that where evidence appears changes how models use it, and positional binding of entities \citep{feng2024bind,prakash2026lookbacks} becomes one of several retrieval mechanisms, and an unreliable one, in longer contexts \citep{gurarieh2026mixing}. A practical consequence for evaluation is that averaging over mention orders can produce null effects that conceal large opposing ones.

Why entity mentions outweigh superseded assignments remains open. The gap persists without the marker and, in the tested templates, when the name--value token gap is matched, although those rewrites also change syntax. The constructions still differ in predicate (\lit{mentioned} versus \lit{was}) and in whether the value is framed as an attribute at all. An update discount, tested in a separate design, is present in Qwen and not detectable in Gemma; because the two follow-ups use different prompts, their estimates are not subtracted from one another.

\paragraph{Limitations.} First, the competence screen is an admission criterion on a separate set of 24 histories; confirmation-set accuracy is reported separately and falls below the screening level for Mistral-7B (98.3\% in one construction), whose contrasts we therefore also report on correctly ranked histories (Section~\ref{sec:additional-models}). Second, model size. Within both families the larger model gives relation status more weight and the entity-mention gap less (Section~\ref{sec:additional-models}). At the sizes tested (4B to 14B parameters), position exceeds relation status in every model and construction does in five of seven; whether relation status overtakes them in larger models is untested and would qualify the main claim. Third, the measure is narrow by design. Scores are bounded masses of fixed answer prefixes, not probabilities of generated answers, and the edited source and donor candidates have low absolute scores; their changes do not imply that probability moves between them or that a generated error becomes likely. The models are at or near ceiling in candidate ranking: Qwen and Gemma rank the current candidate first in every superseded trial, and Mistral-7B in all but 2 of 3,072 (Table~\ref{tab:c-accuracy}); two attempts to lower generated-answer accuracy in Qwen and Gemma failed. With two to six appended distractor entities (Appendix~\ref{app:harder}) and in the development run of update chains up to depth 5 (four updates; Section~\ref{sec:depth}), neither model ever answered a superseded prompt with an earlier value. The 96-history chain run in which Qwen's $R$ rose with depth scored candidates only, so it says nothing about generated answers. In Qwen and Gemma, then, the score shifts do not reach answers. OLMo-2, an exploratory model with lower accuracy outside the screened population, shows a similar qualitative pattern in its generated answers: mentioned and position-aligned values pull them toward the donor, and superseded values almost never surface; its own candidate scores, however, rank superseded above entity-mention relevance. The derived-code task of Appendix~\ref{app:derived-code} brings Qwen's candidate ranking below ceiling (360/384 and 364/384 prompts), but it lacks the Experiment 1 controls and collected no generated answers; a confirmatory test would need a screened model below ceiling, or a task with those controls that brings Qwen and Gemma there, and generated answers. Histories are the bootstrap unit, and names, attributes, templates, the eight- or twelve-value vocabularies, English, and the models are fixed or narrowly sampled, so the intervals do not quantify generalization beyond these stimuli.
