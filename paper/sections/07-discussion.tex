\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The prespecified relation-selective hypothesis predicted that superseded relevance would exceed the other-attribute, entity-mention, and early-unassigned controls. It failed because entity-mention relevance was higher in both models; Gemma also did not show a resolved superseded-minus-other-attribute contrast. This result argues against treating positive $R$ as specific to obsolete assignments, but it does not establish equivalence or show that relation status has no effect. The Qwen average superseded-minus-other-attribute contrast masks opposite signs across attributes. The order-stratified pattern and construction differences described below are secondary descriptive interpretations of the failed prediction, rather than a single confirmatory explanation.

For unassigned values, relevance reverses sign across aligned and reversed order groups in both models. This is consistent with ordinal position linking an unattached value to an entity in these prompts, but it does not identify the internal process. Averaging over mention orders can conceal these opposing effects, as the near-zero overall relevance of unassigned values illustrates. Entity-mention relevance also varies with order, but in opposite directions across models: the paired aligned-minus-reversed difference is −1.969 nats [−2.344, −1.604] in Qwen and +1.810 [1.580, 2.058] in Gemma. The results therefore do not support a universal rule that closer or aligned association uniformly increases sensitivity.

The marker confirmation independently tested adding the explicit prefix \lit{Previously,}; it did not test temporal information versus no temporal information, because the marker-absent superseded sentence remains in past tense. The paired order-averaged entity-mention-minus-superseded gap is positive without the marker in both models, but it varies by order and remains entangled with predicate and syntax. In Gemma's marker-absent reversed-order cell, entity-mention relevance is slightly lower than superseded relevance. Name--value distance is one possible factor for future work, but these data do not test that explanation. The confirmed marker effects show a reduction in the query-specific score contrast after adding the literal prefix, not active suppression of an obsolete representation.

The measure has a deliberately narrow scope. Scores are bounded masses of fixed answer prefixes, not probabilities of generated answers, and both models rank the current value first in every superseded trial. The edited source and donor candidates have low absolute log scores; their score changes do not imply probability conservation between those candidates or a meaningful chance of generated error. In the distractor development follow-up, neither model answered any superseded prompt with the earlier value, and Qwen's accuracy stayed above 99.6\% at all levels, triggering the prespecified stop before test generation (Appendix~\ref{app:harder}). A few Qwen source-value responses occurred in entity-mention controls, where the queried attribute was never assigned that value; these are source-matching answers, not stale-assignment failures. The distractors were appended after the target blocks, each with adjacent historical and current assignments, so this modest manipulation does not establish robustness to generally crowded or conflicting contexts. Histories are the bootstrap sampling unit; the names, attributes, templates, vocabulary, languages, and models remain fixed or narrowly sampled. The intervals do not quantify generalization beyond these stimuli.
