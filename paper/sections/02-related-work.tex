\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Contextual updates and knowledge conflicts.} Entity-tracking benchmarks test whether models recover an entity's state after a sequence of operations \citep{kim2023entity,tang2026entities}, and belief-revision and in-context forgetting studies test whether models revise conclusions or stop using information once new evidence or instructions arrive \citep{wilie2024belief,qian2026forget}. \citet{wang2025unable} show that earlier key--value updates interfere with retrieving the latest value as the number of updates grows, and \citet{guo2026context} characterize stale-answer failures after context updates, including whether the current value remains recoverable and how attention drifts during answer selection. Contradictions between earlier and later statements in one context are intra-context conflicts in the taxonomy of \citet{xu2024conflicts}; \citet{yang2026conflicts} study them and report a consistent bias toward earlier evidence. Most of these benchmarks emphasize answer accuracy; \citet{guo2026context} also probe recoverability and attention, and \citet{yang2026conflicts} analyze evidence attribution. Our estimand is different: the query-specific change in candidate scores caused by a matched edit to an earlier value, measured while the answer is already right, and how it depends on the way that value is mentioned.

\paragraph{Position in context.} Where information sits in the context affects how models use it. \citet{liu2024lost} show that retrieval accuracy depends strongly on the position of the relevant passage in long inputs, and \citet{yang2026conflicts} identify a positional preference as an obstacle to resolving conflicts. Our contexts are six lines long, far from the long-context regime, yet relative position still determines which entity a never-assigned value is credited to.

\paragraph{Entity binding.} Mechanistic studies analyze how models bind entities to attributes in context and retrieve those bindings, including binding-ID representations \citep{feng2024bind}, mixtures of positional, lexical, and reflexive retrieval mechanisms \citep{gurarieh2026mixing}, and lookback mechanisms for tracking characters' beliefs \citep{prakash2026lookbacks}. These works intervene on internal activations. We work at the level of prompts and output scores, which lets us compare how different surface constructions feed whatever binding mechanism the model uses, but not identify the mechanism itself.

\paragraph{Persistence after knowledge editing.} Knowledge-editing studies ask what remains of a fact after it is edited, whether by changing parameters or through an external memory: original facts stay linearly decodable after edits by weight-changing editors and by GRACE, which leaves the weights unchanged and overrides the fact through an external memory \citep{srivastava2026suppressed}, and they can resurface after superficially successful edits \citep{xie2025deceptiveness}. In our setting both the earlier and the current value are present in the input and the model is not edited; we measure sensitivity to an earlier value that is still in view.
