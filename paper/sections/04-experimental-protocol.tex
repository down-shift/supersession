\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Models and competence screen}
\label{sec:models}

We evaluate the instruction-tuned Qwen3-8B and Gemma 3 4B, loaded with int8 weights and float16 (Qwen) or bfloat16 (Gemma) computation, using each model's chat template with thinking disabled for Qwen3. Exact checkpoint revisions are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}.

We restrict the main experiment to models that reliably rank the current value first, so that score sensitivity is studied while candidate selection is correct. A model enters after passing a competence screen on a separate set of 24 histories. In every construction, the correct current value must be strictly ranked first among the eight candidates on at least 99\% of the distinct screening prompts (ties count as errors), and in the superseded construction the mean score margin of the current over the earlier value must be positive. Both models passed. The lowest construction-level accuracies were 287/288 (99.65\%) for Qwen in live and 575/576 (99.83\%) for Gemma in entity mention; both scored 576/576 in superseded, with current-minus-earlier margins of 21.571 and 23.910 nats. Phi-4-mini was also screened and failed in the entity-mention construction (561/576, 97.40\%); it is excluded from the main analyses and reported only descriptively (Section~\ref{sec:followups}).

\subsection{Analysis and prespecification}
\label{sec:inference}

All estimates are computed per history and then averaged over histories; histories are the unit of uncertainty. Intervals are percentile 95\% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of histories (seed 73021). Both models are scored on the same histories, and results are reported per model without pooling.

The design, competence thresholds, and analysis of Experiment 1 were fixed in the project repository before its confirmation data were scored, and a dated amendment recorded before the results were examined specified the decision rule for the original hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:relation}). The 24-history run of Experiment 2 was likewise specified before scoring. The 96-history run of Experiment 2 and the follow-up experiments of Section~\ref{sec:followup-design} were designed and their design documents hash-stamped on the experiment machine before any data were generated, but were not committed to version control. No experiment was externally preregistered. Order-stratified estimates, paired aligned-minus-reversed differences, per-attribute strata, and analyses restricted to correctly answered histories are secondary and exploratory.

\subsection{Experiment 1: constructions crossed with order}
\label{sec:exp1}

Experiment 1 uses 96 histories: each of six entity pairs (Nora/Liam, Ava/Omar, Mila/Eli, Iris/Noah, Zoe/Theo, Maya/Leo) is crossed with the four target attributes and both assignments of names to the $x$ and $z$ roles, with two histories per cell. The other-attribute construction uses \lit{team} in 48 histories and \lit{project} in 48. Each history yields $6\times2\times2\times2\times2\times2=192$ scored prompts: six constructions, two queries, two edited entities, two historical orders, two current orders, and baseline versus edited. This gives 18,432 scored prompts per model. Tokenizer-aware audits verified that each of the 9,216 baseline/edit pairs per model differs only at the intended source/donor span and that both versions of that span are exactly one token, so the unchanged suffix keeps the same token positions.

\subsection{Experiment 2: the explicit marker}
\label{sec:marker-protocol}

In Experiment 1 the superseded and other-attribute constructions carry \lit{Previously} and the others do not. Experiment 2 crosses construction (superseded, entity mention) with marker (absent, present), where adding the marker changes nothing but the prefix \lit{Previously,} on each historical line (Table~\ref{tab:marker-templates}). Historical order, current order, edited entity, query, and baseline versus edit are crossed as in Experiment 1. It uses 96 histories with the Nora/Liam pair and the attribute \lit{badge}, varying the values; they exclude a two-history pilot and an earlier 24-history run of the same design, which is reported as a replication in Appendix~\ref{app:marker}. Each model scores 12,288 prompts (8,544 distinct). The prespecified quantities are the \emph{marker effect}, $R_{\text{present}}-R_{\text{absent}}$, within each construction, and the \emph{interaction}, the superseded marker effect minus the entity-mention marker effect. Because Experiment 2 fixes one entity pair and one attribute, its $R$ values are compared within the experiment rather than with Experiment 1's.

\subsection{Follow-up experiments}
\label{sec:followup-design}

Five follow-ups address open questions left by Experiments 1 and 2. Each was designed, and its design document hash-stamped on the experiment machine, before its data were generated (Section~\ref{sec:inference}); all use the scoring and $R$ analysis above, the same models and configurations, and 2,000 history-bootstrap draws.

\paragraph{Name--value distance.} Entity mentions place the value one word after the name (\lit{Nora mentioned amber}), superseded assignments several tokens later (\lit{Nora's badge was amber}). We crossed construction (superseded, entity mention) with name--value distance (near, far), all lines marked with \lit{Previously}: superseded far \lit{Previously, Nora's badge was amber.}, superseded near \lit{Previously, the badge of Nora was amber.}, entity-mention near \lit{Previously, Nora mentioned amber in an unrelated note.}, and entity-mention far \lit{Previously, Nora, in an unrelated note, mentioned amber.} A tokenizer audit, run before data generation, measured the token gap between name and value: one token in both near conditions for both models, three (Qwen) or four (Gemma) for superseded far, and seven for entity-mention far. The first near superseded wordings we considered (\lit{Nora's badge: amber} and two variants) did not shorten the measured gap and were replaced before any data were generated (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}). The study uses 96 histories with Experiment 1's allocation and 12,288 scored prompts per model. The recorded prediction was that near exceeds far within each construction, with lower 95\% bounds above zero in both constructions.

\paragraph{Updated other attribute.} To separate the two properties joined in superseded versus other attribute, three constructions share a six-line layout: two historical lines, two extra current lines, and the two current lines for the queried attribute. In the superseded construction the historical lines assign the queried attribute and the extra lines assign a filler attribute (\lit{Previously, Nora's badge was amber.}; \lit{Currently, Nora's project is slate.}). In the updated other-attribute construction the historical lines assign another attribute and the extra lines update it (\lit{Previously, Nora's team was amber.}; \lit{Currently, Nora's team is slate.}); in the static other-attribute construction the extra lines assign the filler attribute instead. Superseded minus updated other attribute then measures having been the queried attribute, with both values contradicted, and updated minus static other attribute measures being contradicted by an update, with neither value the queried attribute. The recorded prediction of the discount account was an update effect below zero. The study uses 96 histories with Experiment 1's allocation, all eight vocabulary values per history, and 9,216 scored prompts per model.

\paragraph{Update chains.} To test generated answers below ceiling, each entity received a chain of three, four, or five values revealed in rounds (\lit{Initially, \ldots was v.}, \lit{Then, \ldots changed to v.}, \lit{Currently, \ldots is v.}); the edit replaced the immediately superseded value, and the vocabulary was extended to twelve values chosen by a recorded tokenizer audit. Models generated answers greedily and were also scored on candidate masses. A development run of 12 histories per depth was to select the shallowest depth at which at least one model's answer accuracy fell between 70\% and 95\%, and a 96-history confirmation would then measure whether edits move generated answers toward the donor value; if no depth qualified, the study would stop. Because no depth qualified, a second run scored candidate masses only on 96 fresh histories per depth, to estimate how $R$ changes with depth; the recorded rule counted growth as supported if $R$ at depth 5 minus $R$ at depth 3 had a lower 95\% bound above zero, comparing independent history sets.

\paragraph{Generated answers below ceiling (exploratory).} Because Qwen and Gemma answer at ceiling, generated answers were studied in OLMo-2-7B-Instruct, which fails the competence screen (Section~\ref{sec:followups}) and so lies outside the screened population. It generated answers greedily (at most 32 tokens, exact match to the eight values) on all 18,432 Experiment 1 confirmation prompts. The generated-answer analogue of $R$ is \emph{donor following}: the share of edited prompts answered with the donor when the edited entity is queried, minus that share when the other entity is queried, computed per history. This study is exploratory and does not enter any confirmatory claim.

\paragraph{Additional models.} OLMo-2-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-14B were to be run through Experiment 1 on its sealed screening and confirmation datasets with the same renderer, scorer, competence screen, and analysis; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, originally planned, was unavailable because access to its gated weights was declined. Phi-4-mini, which failed the screen, was scored on the confirmation dataset descriptively.
