\section{Follow-up experiment details}
\label{app:followups}

The follow-ups of Section~\ref{sec:followup-design} were run on the RTX 5080 used for the main experiments, with the same pinned checkpoints, int8 configurations, and scoring code. Each design document was hash-stamped on the experiment machine before its data were generated (not committed to version control; Section~\ref{sec:inference}). Artifact hashes are listed in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Name--value distance}

\paragraph{Design.} Entity mentions place the value one word after the name (\lit{Nora mentioned amber}), superseded assignments several tokens later (\lit{Nora's badge was amber}). We crossed construction (superseded, entity mention) with name--value distance (near, far), all lines marked with \lit{Previously}: superseded far \lit{Previously, Nora's badge was amber.}, superseded near \lit{Previously, the badge of Nora was amber.}, entity-mention near \lit{Previously, Nora mentioned amber in an unrelated note.}, and entity-mention far \lit{Previously, Nora, in an unrelated note, mentioned amber.} A tokenizer audit, run before data generation, measured the token gap between name and value: one token in both near conditions for both models, three (Qwen) or four (Gemma) for superseded far, and seven for entity-mention far. The first near superseded wordings we considered (\lit{Nora's badge: amber} and two variants) did not shorten the measured gap and were replaced before any data were generated (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}). The study uses 96 histories with Experiment 1's allocation and 12,288 scored prompts per model. The recorded prediction was that near exceeds far within each construction, with lower 95\% bounds above zero in both constructions.

\paragraph{Choice of the near superseded template.} The design listed three near superseded wordings (\lit{Nora's badge: amber}, \lit{Nora's badge = amber}, \lit{Nora's badge, amber}) and a rule choosing the first whose baseline/edit pairs differ only in the edited value's tokens for both tokenizers. A tokenizer audit run before data generation found that all three pass that check but leave the measured name--value gap unchanged (three tokens in Qwen, four in Gemma, as for the far template), so the superseded arm would not have varied distance. Before any data were generated, an amendment added a fourth candidate, \lit{Previously, the badge of Nora was amber.}, and required the chosen template to be measurably nearer than the far template in both tokenizers; the re-run audit chose it, with a gap of one token in both. The amendment was hash-stamped separately.

\paragraph{Data.} 96 histories (Experiment 1's allocation of entity pairs, attributes, and name orientations), excluding all histories of the marker pilot and the 24-history marker run; 12,288 scored prompts per model (9,152 distinct); scoring took 6,472 s (Qwen) and 2,656 s (Gemma). Results are in Table~\ref{tab:distance}; the distance-by-construction interaction (superseded near-minus-far minus entity-mention near-minus-far) was 0.871 [0.703, 1.040] in Qwen and 0.420 [0.291, 0.542] in Gemma.

\subsection{Update chains}

\paragraph{Design.} Chain depth is the total number of successive assignments of the queried attribute, including the current one, so depth 3 means two updates. To test generated answers below ceiling, each entity received a chain of three, four, or five assignments revealed in rounds (\lit{Initially, \ldots was v.}, \lit{Then, \ldots changed to v.}, \lit{Currently, \ldots is v.}); the edit replaced the immediately superseded value, and the vocabulary was extended to twelve values chosen by a recorded tokenizer audit. Models generated answers greedily and were also scored on candidate masses. A development run of 12 histories per depth was to select the shallowest depth at which at least one model's answer accuracy fell between 70\% and 95\%, and a 96-history confirmation would then measure whether edits move generated answers toward the donor value; if no depth qualified, the study would stop. Because no depth qualified, a second run scored candidate masses only on 96 fresh histories per depth, to estimate how $R$ changes with depth; the recorded rule counted growth as supported if $R$ at depth 5 minus $R$ at depth 3 had a lower 95\% bound above zero, comparing independent history sets. Development: 384 generated answers per depth and model.

\paragraph{Vocabulary.} Disjoint chains of up to five values per entity plus two donors need twelve values. The design extended the eight-value vocabulary by walking a recorded candidate list in order and keeping each value that is not a substring of another and keeps the candidate continuations valid for both tokenizers; the audit kept the first four candidates, \lit{olive}, \lit{plum}, \lit{navy}, and \lit{rust}.

\paragraph{Development.} 12 histories per depth (32 prompts per history: historical order $\times$ current order $\times$ edited entity $\times$ query $\times$ baseline/edit), greedy decoding with at most 32 new tokens, and the exact-answer parser of Appendix~\ref{app:harder}. Table~\ref{tab:f-chains} reports accuracy, the share of answers naming any earlier value, and $R$ from candidate masses over the twelve values. Every answer was correct for both models at every depth, so the recorded selection rule chose no depth and the confirmation was not generated.

\subsection{Updated other attribute}

\paragraph{Design.} To separate the two properties joined in superseded versus other attribute, three constructions share a six-line layout: two historical lines, two extra current lines, and the two current lines for the queried attribute. In the superseded construction the historical lines assign the queried attribute and the extra lines assign a filler attribute (\lit{Previously, Nora's badge was amber.}; \lit{Currently, Nora's project is slate.}). In the updated other-attribute construction the historical lines assign another attribute and the extra lines update it (\lit{Previously, Nora's team was amber.}; \lit{Currently, Nora's team is slate.}); in the static other-attribute construction the extra lines assign the filler attribute instead. Superseded minus updated other attribute then measures having been the queried attribute, with both values contradicted, and updated minus static other attribute measures being contradicted by an update, with neither value the queried attribute. The recorded prediction of the discount account was an update effect below zero. The study uses 96 histories with Experiment 1's allocation, all eight vocabulary values per history, and 9,216 scored prompts per model.

Restricting both models to histories in which every prompt is ranked correctly, as in Table~\ref{tab:b-selected}, leaves 87 histories for Qwen and 28 for Gemma. The queried-attribute effects remain positive and resolved, Qwen's update effect remains negative and resolved, and Gemma's update effect remains unresolved. Gemma's restricted estimates rest on fewer than a third of its histories.

\subsection{Generated answers below ceiling (exploratory)}

Generated answers are summarized by \emph{donor following}, a separate descriptive measure computed on edited prompts only, without baseline adjustment: the share of edited prompts answered with the donor when the edited entity is queried, minus that share when the other entity is queried, computed per history. This study is exploratory and does not enter any confirmatory claim.

\subsection{Additional models}

\paragraph{Procedure.} Each additional model was scored on Experiment 1's sealed screening dataset (24 histories) and, if it passed, on the sealed confirmation dataset (96 histories), with the same renderer, candidate validation, scorer, competence rule, and analysis code as Qwen and Gemma. All additional models were loaded with int8 weights at pinned revisions. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the planned model from a third family, could not be used because access to its gated weights was declined; Falcon3-7B-Instruct and Granite-3.1-8B-Instruct were added as models from other families, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Gemma 3 12B as other generations and sizes within the two original families. Qwen3-14B did not fit in 16 GB of GPU memory and was run on an 80 GB GPU; Qwen2.5-7B produced non-finite logits with float16 computation and was run with bfloat16 computation, before any of its scores were written. Granite's chat template inserts the current date into every prompt; the rendered prompts are stored with its scores.

OLMo-2-7B-Instruct (revision \lit{470b1fba1ae01581f270116362ee4aa1b97f4c84}, bfloat16 computation) failed in four constructions.
